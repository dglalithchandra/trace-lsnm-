# TRACE — final order of work

Decision for the ZEISS BeyondCode retinal-imaging challenge, 11 November 2026. This is a proposed work plan. The results quoted below come from completed TRACE development experiments.

## What to build

Deliver one reusable colour-fundus encoder trained with label-hidden contrastive learning. A new task supplies a few labelled example eyes. TRACE ranks new images, retrieves comparable examples and records image quality, score reliability and the provenance of each example. The central experiment asks whether the encoder learns more transferable pathology when two valid views preserve small lesions and visually similar eyes receive fewer erroneous training penalties.

Slides 6–7 of the organizer brief assign 25% to hidden transfer, 20% to few-shot/linear probing, 20% to contrastive innovation, 20% to trust/OOD and 15% to efficiency, interpretability and reproducibility. They specify RFMiD pretraining with labels hidden, organizer-defined adaptation, held-out RFMiD and IDRiD, and hidden quality shifts. They ask for an encoder, code, model card, UMAP, retrieval and ablations. Additional datasets assume the external-data permission reported by the team. Official manifests and instructions take precedence for the submission.

## Decisions grounded in TRACE results

- **Keep the camera module.** Its 448-pixel geometry, mask, quality measurements and audit output provide the input interface. Earlier colour correction failed its joint disease and domain criteria. Learned glare QC rejected too many clean images. Reopen either only when independent evidence changes that decision.
- **Build a contrastively trained encoder.** The rejected supervised residual adapter improved base-label development scores but changed 3-shot novel-label macro average precision from 0.1988 to 0.1774. That result concerns supervision on ten base labels. It leaves the label-hidden contrastive hypothesis open. Published work already adapts DINOv2 to fundus images and evaluates few-shot detection of rare fundus pathologies. The candidate contribution is the measured effect of the pairing rule across diseases and acquisitions. [DINORET](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.17332) · [Quellec et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32028213/)
- **Omit the diagnostic knowledge graph from the submission.** The encoder currently supplies embeddings and disease scores. A graph would need independent patient-level findings to contribute new evidence. The project has no clinical adjudicator for inferred findings. Assign the graph workstream to a label dictionary and a retrieval evaluation. A future graph could be tested if verified finding inputs become available.

## Order of work

### 1. Freeze the baseline, the data boundaries and the question

Version the code, environment, current checkpoint, preprocessing, dataset manifests and historical results. Log all accesses to protected sets. Mark v1 training/validation, PALM, FIVES and Paraguay with their actual prior uses. Record the earlier IDRiD development geometry/mask audit and RFMiD–IDRiD overlap. Preserve genuinely untouched records and use organizer-approved support splits at adaptation time. Record whether each candidate model's pretraining included, possibly included, or had no documented exposure to each evaluation set. The friend's model has a different training pool, including RFMiD 2.0 training images. Its supplied metrics belong in a contextual comparison until an eligible checkpoint runs on identical episodes.

### 2. Give each dataset one purpose before importing it

| Dataset | Role | Admission or interpretation rule |
|---|---|---|
| RFMiD v1 | Primary SSL training on approved training photographs. Development splits for model and head selection. Official holdout for final transfer. | Strip diagnostic CSV fields from the SSL loader and prevent access to validation/test pixels during pretraining. Preserve a distinct set of development classes for head tuning. |
| IDRiD | Organizer-specified few-shot/hidden transfer, plus only the adaptation labels the organizers release | Quarantine duplicates from training. Report prior development access precisely. DR/DME results answer a grading or transfer question. |
| EyePACS | First choice for an additional heterogeneous unlabeled training pool when the team secures suitable access. Run RFMiD-only and pooled training at matched update counts. | Deduplicate across datasets, balance source sampling, record permissions for the actual release and publish counts. One large DR-screening source can dominate the learned representation. |
| RFMiD 2.0 | First new external evaluation dataset. Draw support from v1 and score disjoint v2 queries for labels with equivalent definitions. | Release has 860 images. The paper names two cameras at different sites. Validate per-image assignments before reporting device transfer. Paper and repository differ on category count, so inspect CSV semantics through an outcome-blinded data audit. [Dataset](https://zenodo.org/records/7505822) · [Data paper](https://www.mdpi.com/2306-5729/8/2/29) |
| JSIEC | Local fallback external multi-condition cohort or secondary replication for exact mapped labels | Audit the archive, label completeness, overlap and permissions. A single category assigned to an image can leave other conditions unknown. [Official release](https://zenodo.org/records/3477553) |
| PALM, Paraguay, FIVES | Existing device, site/calibration and quality stress development studies, respectively | Report reuse. Existing results make these valuable development probes. |
| Paired Retina | Candidate for a matched tabletop-versus-portable evaluation of DR score stability | Authors describe 327 patients and the viewer lists 399 rows. Verify actual pairing keys, eye, visit, real-image provenance, labels and independent patient count before use. Limit the inference to the device pair and task studied. [Author data card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/smartretina2025/paired_retina) |

For a clinically rare disease extension, consider **Birdshot-WIDE** if controlled access is granted. It supplies a named rare diagnosis, widefield images and matched controls. Use a dedicated field-preserving input audit and patient-grouped splits across visits and eyes. Report the rare-disease-versus-healthy task separately from conventional fundus multi-disease ranking. If access fails, qualify the RFMiD endpoint as low-label and source-document any claim about clinical rarity. [Birdshot-WIDE data paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13242503/)

### 3. Build TRACE-Bench v2 and preregister the decision

Your friend's first deliverable is a small table mapping original disease names and definitions to exact, broader, narrower, ambiguous and unavailable matches, with source locators and review status. Count independent positives and valid negatives by label, source and device. Audit exact duplicates and resized/cropped copies using fine retinal detail. Split by patient where identifiers exist, otherwise keep related-image groups together and disclose residual dependence.

Choose a primary external cohort using outcome-blinded feasibility criteria before comparing models. Predefine minimum unique cases using development-only precision simulations. If the external rare-label cohort cannot support an informative comparison, keep it descriptive, report which labels failed eligibility and identify the protected internal test as the feasible primary endpoint before unblinding. Use different people/groups for support and queries. More support draws improve estimates of variability across examples. More independent positive eyes improve the underlying evidence.

Proposed primary endpoint is **3-shot macro average precision (AP)** at the natural query prevalence on eligible held-out labels. Count both positive and negative labels supplied during adaptation. Keep the original abundant-negative and strict K-positive/K-negative protocols distinct. Report 1- and 5-shot secondarily, 10-shot where supported, per-label AP and prevalence, AUROC, and sensitivity/specificity at the operating threshold fixed on development. Estimate paired intervals using common support draws, shared patient/query resamples across models and labels, and record/group-level resampling where required. Predefine the superiority effect of interest and clean-performance non-inferiority margin from development and practical impact. Simulate attainable precision before locking them. A wide interval means the experiment was inconclusive. Preserve the organizers' scoring definition alongside the research endpoint.

### 4. Establish the comparators on identical development episodes

Use a supervised ImageNet ResNet-50 and plain MoCo as controls. Compare frozen DINOv2-S, existing DINOv2-L and RETFound-DINOv2, and add frozen DINOv3-L. Include RetiZero as a vision-language comparator if its released weights and terms support the intended evaluation. Report possible public-dataset exposure for RetiZero and FLAIR. Cache features and use identical query IDs, label budgets and selection opportunities. Use each model's documented input normalization. Compare pretrained weights with actual adaptation separately. Choose the strongest eligible frozen comparator on development and carry DINOv2-S as the efficiency reference. [Specialist/generalist study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03421) · [DINOv3 implementation](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dinov3) · [Official RetiZero release](https://github.com/LooKing9218/RetiZero)

### 5. Train one challenge-specific contrastive encoder

Use DINOv2-S/14 at 448 px as the initial student because TRACE already has an audited input pipeline, frozen features and same-backbone baseline. Train LoRA updates in the upper attention blocks and a small projection head with a momentum teacher and an InfoNCE objective. Export the adapted image encoder. The teacher, queue and projection head are training machinery. Compare total weights, trainable weights, training cost and inference cost. Scale to a stronger backbone only when a matched development comparison justifies it.

1. Form positive pairs from two nearly full-retina views of each photograph. Track coordinates for mild rotation/translation and retain both the disc and peripheral retina. Bound exposure changes. Validate mask retention plus local lesion contrast/visibility on disjoint development examples with annotations. An independent potential source for microaneurysm and exudate masks is [e-ophtha](https://www.adcis.net/en/third-party/e-ophtha/).
2. Use source-balanced sampling and a negative queue. Different photos can share disease. Test down-weighting a capped fraction of reciprocal teacher neighbours that recur across mild views and come from different verified acquisition groups or sources. Disable that cross-source rule when metadata are unknown. Keep SSL label-blind. Audit removed neighbours on a separate labelled development subset for shared findings, camera enrichment and common anatomy. False-negative cancellation has prior art. [Huynh et al.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2022/html/Huynh_Boosting_Contrastive_Self-Supervised_Learning_With_False_Negative_Cancellation_WACV_2022_paper.html)
3. Match backbone, training images, resolution, updates and head budget across plain contrastive training, preservation-constrained views, and those views plus neighbour down-weighting. Include a random down-weighting control matched in number and weight. Repeat finalists across at least three training seeds. Track representation collapse, disease transfer, clean sensitivity, camera/source probe performance after case-mix controls and stress sensitivity. A weaker camera probe alone earns no win.
4. Test one additional patch-level consistency term only if annotated development cases demonstrate a local-information failure. Match the same visible retinal region after the known geometric transform. Treat severe glare, missing fields and disappearing lesions as stress tests. They can remove the evidence needed for a positive pair.

### 6. Prove downstream adaptation and interpretable retrieval

For each finalist run a frozen linear probe, a 1/3/5-shot regularized logistic head against nearest-prototype, and a separate label-budget-matched supervised PEFT evaluation. Reuse fixed support draws. The 45-output RFMiD head remains tied to its original embedding space. Refit any downstream head on the allowed training embeddings when the encoder changes.

Your friend's second deliverable is an index of permitted labelled support images with source, split, quality and duplicate group. For a query, return similar examples from other eyes, their recorded labels, similarity and source. Evaluate precision@5 for matched findings where labels are reliable, same-source bias and failure examples. Show a UMAP coloured separately by disease and acquisition source alongside the quantitative measures. An optional local-patch retrieval ablation can investigate a specific lesion-scale failure. Retrieved examples provide inspectable comparisons. Similarity scores require separate calibration before a probability interpretation.

### 7. Evaluate trust and practical efficiency

Use the previous Paraguay finding to anchor a prespecified exploratory label-budget experiment. The frozen DR score ranked cases with AUROC 0.888 but sensitivity at the transferred threshold was 0.304. Compare source-only thresholds, target threshold selection and a simple local calibrator with disjoint calibration/evaluation images, a range of label budgets and uncertainty. Report any sensitivity gain together with specificity, Brier score and risk versus coverage. Count all labels used. This study provides evidence about DR at one site.

Measure unfamiliar-input detection separately from predicting individual errors. Include genuine held-out acquisition domains, synthetic quality shifts, and natural variations in quality. Compare simple confidence and embedding-distance baselines, false alarm rate and retained sensitivity, including missed positives at high confidence. Benchmark batch-one latency, memory, training compute and checkpoint size under documented conditions.

### 8. Lock, confirm once, and submit

Select one trained candidate and one primary comparator using development data. Freeze hashes, thresholds, projections, label mappings, support policy, analysis and reporting code. Run the protected internal and external comparisons once under the agreed split policy. Use organizer hidden data only through their authorized workflow. Report failed gates, unavailable metrics, uncertainty, label counts and prior data exposure. Select a new primary model only if the prespecified transfer and preservation gates pass. If they fail, deliver the trained research checkpoint and clearly identify which eligible baseline performed best.

Package one callable image-to-embedding interface, preprocessing, example adaptation and retrieval, code/environment and manifests, checkpoints or authorized download instructions, model card, UMAP, ablation table, trust plots and an auditable research demo. The demo should show a query, support examples, ranking and quality warning with provenance. The report's claim follows the evaluated conditions. Training the encoder, proving transfer, and establishing performance for a clinically rare condition are three separate achievements.
